01The approved one

What Melanotan 1 actually is

Melanotan 1 is afamelanotide — same molecule, two names. It’s a synthetic, long-acting copy of the body’s pigment hormone (α-MSH), engineered to be selective for the pigment receptor. Approved as Scenesse, it’s a prescription implant for a rare light-sensitivity disease — not a tanning product.

Each bead is one amino acid. The highlighted tweaks turn natural α-MSH into a long-acting drug.

Three names, one molecule

Melanotan 1 (research name) = afamelanotide (generic) = Scenesse (brand). One 13-amino-acid peptide with an identity crisis — and very different reputations per name.

Selective by design

Unlike MT2’s shotgun, afamelanotide is biased toward MC1R, the pigment receptor. Fewer receptors hit means fewer whole-body surprises — the selectivity that made approval possible.

A disease drug, not a tan drug

Approved for erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP) — a rare genetic disease where light causes excruciating pain. It’s given as a prescription implant placed by a clinician, not sold for cosmetic tanning.

Plain English

If MT2 is a skeleton key, afamelanotide is a key cut for one lock: the pigment switch. Same key blank, wildly different key — and only one of them earned a medical license, for a disease where sunlight feels like being burned from the inside.

02Light hurts

The disease behind the drug

Scenesse exists because of EPP — one of the most painful rare diseases you’ve never heard of. Understanding the disease explains why regulators said yes here and no to cosmetic tanning.

The selective sibling took the medical path: decades of trials for one rare disease.
EPP: when light burns

Erythropoietic protoporphyria is a genetic disorder where a porphyrin builds up in the blood. Minutes of sunlight trigger agonizing, burning pain lasting days — no blisters, no rash, just pain. Patients live like vampires by necessity.

Melanin as armor

More eumelanin means more built-in light protection. In EPP trials, afamelanotide increased melanin density and — crucially — pain-free time in sunlight, the outcome that mattered.

Why approval made sense

For a disease with no other treatment and life-altering pain, regulators accepted the trade-offs: nausea, moles darkening, implant-site effects. The benefit–harm math that fails for cosmetic tanning passes for EPP.

Plain English

Context is everything in medicine: the same tan that’s a frivolity at the beach is armor for someone whose blood turns sunlight into agony. Regulators didn’t approve a tanning drug — they approved pain-free daylight.

04Evidence check

What the science actually supports

A real approval file: randomized trials in EPP, measured outcomes, regulatory review on two continents. The honest limits are about scope, not quality.

EPP trials: positive

Randomized, controlled trials showed more pain-free time in sunlight and higher melanin density vs. control. The endpoint was patient-relevant, not cosmetic.

Two-continent approval

EU 2014, US 2019 — regulators on both sides reviewed the full file and said yes for EPP. That’s the strongest evidence signal a drug can carry.

Narrow by design

The evidence covers EPP only: small patient numbers (it’s a rare disease), and no data for cosmetic tanning, libido, or anything else the internet speculates about.

Plain English

This is the rare page where the evidence chapter is boring in the best way: trials asked a clear question, got a clear answer, regulators agreed — twice. The file isn’t thin; it’s just narrow, covering one rare disease and nothing else.

05MT1 vs MT2

Afamelanotide vs Melanotan 2

The comparison the internet constantly mangles. Same family, same tanning idea — different molecules, different selectivity, different legal and medical universes.

Afamelanotide · the scalpel

Linear 13-aa, MC1R-selective. Approved as Scenesse (EU 2014, US 2019) for EPP. Prescription implant, clinician-placed. Side effects: nausea, headache, mole darkening.

Melanotan 2 · the shotgun

Cyclic 7-aa, non-selective (MC1R/3R/4R/5R). Never approved anywhere. Gray-market vials and sprays. Bundle: tan plus nausea, appetite loss, erections, mole changes.

Why it matters

Forums constantly blur the two — “melanotan” almost always means MT2. Borrowing afamelanotide’s approved halo for MT2’s unregulated vial is the most common category error in tanning-peptide discourse.

One molecule earned approval through selectivity and trials; the other lives on forums.
Plain English

Calling MT2 “just like Scenesse” is like calling moonshine “just like prescription medicine” because both contain alcohol. Same chemical family, opposite universes of testing, oversight, and legal status — learn the two names.

07Risks & unknowns

Risks & unknowns

An approved drug still has a risk file — it’s just a studied one. What the label says, what’s plausible, and what remains unknown.

Nausea & headache

Labeled: nausea and headache are the most common adverse effects — typically transient, and milder than MT2’s GI reputation.

Pigmentation changes

Labeled: darkening of skin, moles, and freckles. EPP patients get regular skin monitoring — the label treats mole vigilance as part of the therapy.

Implant-site effects

Labeled: pain, swelling, or irritation where the implant is placed. A clinician procedure with clinician-managed aftercare.

The melanoma question

Theoretical: stimulating melanocytes long-term raises the textbook question. No signal has emerged in EPP surveillance, but the monitored population is small — absence of evidence, carefully watched.

Decades of use

Unknown: EPP is lifelong; decades-long safety data is still accumulating. What very-long-term melanocyte stimulation does is being learned in real time, under supervision.

Off-label & counterfeit

Unknown: “afamelanotide” from unregulated sellers is frequently not afamelanotide at all. Anything outside the prescription implant is a different risk universe.

Plain English

Known: nausea, pigmentation shifts, implant-site effects — all labeled, all monitored. Theoretical: the long-term melanocyte question, watched but unanswered. Unknown: decades-out data and whatever’s in counterfeit vials. A real risk file — which is precisely what approval buys you.
